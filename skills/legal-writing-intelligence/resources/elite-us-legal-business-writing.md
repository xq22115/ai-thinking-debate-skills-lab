# Elite US legal-business correspondence logic

This package emulates **public professional conventions used in high-stakes US legal and executive correspondence**, not the personal voice of any named lawyer. The goal is to reproduce the logic that makes a letter feel partner-level: issue control, option preservation, precise asks, calibrated pressure, and a defensible record.

## The hidden layer is signaling, not secret code

Experienced lawyers routinely use compact phrases that do legal/commercial work beyond their literal words. Treat them as switches with preconditions, not decorations.

| Signal | What it does | Use when | Do not misuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| `For present purposes` | Narrows the proposition to the immediate issue | You want to avoid conceding broader points | Do not use to hide a material fact |
| `For the avoidance of doubt` | Removes an ambiguity before it becomes leverage | A term, deadline or responsibility can be read two ways | Overuse makes writing defensive |
| `We do not accept the premise that …` | Rejects an opponent's framing without debating every detail | The framing itself creates the wrong burden | Follow with your controlling frame |
| `Subject to …` | Makes a position conditional | Approval, diligence, definitive documents, financing, permits, board action | Conditions must be real and intelligible |
| `Without waiving …` / `All rights are reserved` | Preserves legal positions while discussing a practical path | You are negotiating or accommodating without intending surrender | It is not a magic cure for conduct that actually waives a right |
| `Nothing herein constitutes an admission` | Separates discussion from factual/legal concession | Disputed events, settlement positioning, remedial action | Do not use to make false statements |
| `On the current record` | Ties the conclusion to presently known evidence | Facts may develop | State what would change the view if material |
| `We would be prepared to …` | Signals controlled flexibility without promising | You can offer a path subject to defined conditions | Avoid if you lack authority |
| `Please confirm by [date/time]` | Converts discussion into an auditable decision point | A next action or deadline matters | Give a realistic deadline and timezone |
| `In the interim` | Sets temporary conduct while the main issue is unresolved | Project continuity, preservation, no-change instructions | Avoid accidental new obligations |

## Partner-level architecture

1. **One-line purpose.** State why the letter exists and the decision required.
2. **Controlling frame.** Define the commercial/legal issue in your terms before discussing details.
3. **Record, not narrative.** Use only decision-relevant facts, dates, contract sections, approvals, amounts, milestones and prior notices.
4. **Position.** State the conclusion cleanly. Separate fact, interpretation, and requested action.
5. **Optionality.** Preserve non-waiver, approvals, diligence, and other genuine conditions without creating fake ambiguity.
6. **Action package.** Ask for one or more concrete acts, owners, dates, delivery method, and required evidence.
7. **Escalation ladder.** Make the next consequence legible without theatrical threats: meeting → executive escalation → contractual notice → formal remedy.
8. **Close the record.** Identify what remains open, what is not conceded, and the exact confirmation required.

## Executive / international-project mode

For major construction, infrastructure, energy, M&A, procurement, financing or cross-border work, prefer:

- quantified milestones over adjectives;
- defined dependencies and critical-path impacts;
- owner/action/date/evidence tuples;
- contract-clause references only where they change the decision;
- neutral descriptions of failure, followed by consequence;
- proposed cure path before remedy escalation when commercially sensible;
- explicit preservation of rights if cooperation could otherwise be misread.

The tone is **controlled, specific, economical, and reversible**. Avoid insults, melodrama, pseudo-Latin, empty aggression, excessive `hereby`/`heretofore`, and legalese that does not change legal effect.

## Privilege / settlement labels

Do not automatically stamp `Privileged & Confidential`, `Attorney Work Product`, or `Without Prejudice`. Labels do not by themselves create privilege, work-product protection, or settlement exclusion. The writer must know the jurisdiction, audience, purpose and underlying facts before applying them.
