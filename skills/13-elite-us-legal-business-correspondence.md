# Elite U.S. Legal & Business Correspondence Skill

Use this skill for high-stakes business correspondence, in-house counsel communications, transaction letters, formal dispute positions, and international engineering/project correspondence where the user wants the compressed, credible feel of experienced U.S. commercial counsel.

## Objective

Produce correspondence that reads as professionally sophisticated because its **logic, evidence discipline, commitment control and procedural awareness** are sophisticated — not because it copies a named lawyer, adds archaic legalese, or inserts impressive-sounding labels.

The operative model is:

> decision → record → verified authority → analysis → commercial/project effect → precise ask → deadline/next step → narrow reservation if actually required

For international construction/project matters, add the causal claim chain:

> project reference → chronology → verified notice/contract basis → cause → effect → entitlement → quantum → requested instruction/determination → deadline → contemporaneous records

## What “insider language” really means

Do **not** treat professional legal correspondence as a dictionary of secret phrases. The high-signal patterns are functional:

1. **Decision-first compression.** A senior reader sees the position, recommendation or requested decision before the history.
2. **Record anchoring.** Important factual assertions are tied to dates, communications, instructions, meeting records, contract references or other contemporaneous evidence.
3. **Authority anchoring.** Legal/contractual positions are tied to verified clauses, cases, statutes, policies, precedents or institutional playbooks.
4. **Calibrated commitment.** Make clear whether language is an inquiry, proposal, present position, conditional willingness, instruction or final commitment.
5. **Controlled conditionality.** Put conditions beside the proposal they qualify. Do not hide material conditions in the closing paragraph.
6. **Risk allocation.** State the practical consequence and who owns the next decision without unnecessary accusation.
7. **Escalation posture.** Deliberately distinguish cooperative clarification, executive escalation, formal notice, dispute preservation and settlement dialogue.
8. **Explicit close.** One clear action, owner and deadline or next procedural step.

These patterns create the “an experienced person wrote this” effect more reliably than fake Latin, inflated vocabulary, or copied boilerplate.

## Mandatory intake

Before drafting, reconstruct these fields from the user’s material. If a field is missing and materially affects legal effect, mark it for verification rather than guessing:

- `objective`: what outcome should this correspondence produce?
- `audience`: counterparty executive, outside counsel, in-house counsel, Engineer, Employer, Contractor, JV partner, board, regulator, etc.
- `known_facts`: only facts supported by the user or supplied records.
- `matter/project_reference`: matter number, agreement, package, PO, variation, instruction, claim, correspondence reference.
- `contract_anchors`: exact verified provisions if supplied.
- `legal_authorities`: exact verified authorities if supplied.
- `requested_action`: the decision, instruction, cure, confirmation, information or payment requested.
- `deadline`: only if supplied or independently verified.
- `commitment_state`: inquiry / proposal / present position / conditional willingness / firm instruction / final commitment.
- `legal_labels_confirmed`: only labels whose legal/factual basis has actually been confirmed.

For international projects also collect:

- governing form/version and amendments;
- required notice addressee and communication method;
- relevant instruction/change/event;
- cause;
- time/cost/scope effect;
- entitlement theory;
- valuation/quantum status;
- programme/site/cost/contemporaneous records;
- any contractual time bar or response period.

## Four drafting modes

### 1. Executive Counsel

Use for CEO/GC/board/executive correspondence where the reader needs a decision, not a memo.

Order:

1. bottom-line position or recommendation;
2. two-to-five controlling facts;
3. verified governing premise;
4. risk/commercial consequence;
5. recommended action;
6. owner + deadline/next step.

Style: calm, compressed, decisive. Short paragraphs. One material idea per paragraph. Do not bury the recommendation.

### 2. Transactional

Use for negotiations, term issues, redlines, counterproposals and deal correspondence.

Order:

1. commercial objective;
2. current proposal state;
3. conditions and reciprocity;
4. allocation of risk / drafting issue;
5. precise counterproposal or drafting instruction;
6. next drafting step.

Make the commitment state explicit. Useful patterns may include wording such as “Our present position is…”, “We would be prepared to consider… provided that…”, or “Please confirm whether…”, but only when those phrases accurately express the user’s intended commitment.

### 3. Dispute Preservation

Use for a contested position or threatened dispute where the record matters.

Order:

1. identify the disputed proposition precisely;
2. state the relevant chronology and contemporaneous record;
3. state the verified legal/contractual basis;
4. explain the measured consequence;
5. state the cure/response/decision required;
6. state the procedural deadline if verified;
7. use only a rights reservation or legal label whose basis has been confirmed.

Attack the proposition and record, not motive or character. Do not turn firmness into hostility.

### 4. International Project

Use for large engineering, EPC, infrastructure, FIDIC-style or similar cross-border project communications.

Order:

1. project/contract/package/correspondence reference;
2. purpose and present position;
3. chronology;
4. verified communication/notice/contract basis;
5. cause;
6. effect;
7. entitlement;
8. quantum/valuation status;
9. instruction/determination/information requested;
10. response deadline;
11. contemporaneous records and only the reservation actually required.

Treat `cause → effect → entitlement → quantum` as a proof chain. Do not collapse “an event occurred” into “therefore money/time is automatically owed.”

## Fail-closed legal-effect controls

The following are **not sophistication decorations** and must never be inserted automatically:

- `without prejudice`;
- `for settlement purposes only`;
- `subject to contract`;
- `attorney-client privileged`;
- `attorney work product`;
- `all rights reserved`.

Enable them only if the user confirms the applicable basis or the governing jurisdiction/contract has been verified. A header does not create privilege by itself. A generic rights reservation does not replace a contractual notice, required addressee, delivery method, content requirement or time bar.

Never invent:

- clause numbers;
- cases/statutes;
- contract language;
- publication dates;
- prior approvals;
- correspondence history;
- privilege status;
- deadlines;
- damages/quantum;
- factual admissions.

If authority is missing, carry the literal marker:

`[VERIFY CONTRACT / LEGAL AUTHORITY]`

until a source supplies it.

## Writing mechanics

- Put the most important substantive point first.
- Prefer active voice when it identifies responsibility accurately.
- Use defined terms only when they reduce ambiguity.
- Keep adjectives out unless they change the legal/commercial meaning.
- Separate verified fact, inference, legal conclusion and commercial recommendation.
- Prefer a concrete date/document reference over “as you know,” “clearly,” “obviously,” or rhetorical emphasis.
- Use headings when a message contains multiple decision points, but do not turn a short executive note into a litigation brief.
- If the recipient can reasonably misunderstand whether the sender is accepting, proposing, reserving, instructing or merely asking, rewrite.

## Evidence and precedent hierarchy

For a real matter, prefer this order:

1. user-provided signed contract / amendment / governing record;
2. user or organization approved precedent/playbook;
3. current primary legal/official authority;
4. matter history and contemporaneous records;
5. reputable practitioner guidance for technique;
6. generic model wording only as scaffolding.

Do not use popularity or a model’s confidence as a substitute for authority.

## Release checklist

A finished high-stakes draft is not ready until all applicable answers are “yes”:

- Is the desired decision/action obvious from the opening and close?
- Can each material fact be traced to a supplied record?
- Is every authority/contract anchor verified rather than invented?
- Is the sender’s commitment state unambiguous?
- Are material conditions next to the proposal they qualify?
- Is the escalation posture deliberate?
- For project notices, were addressee, form, method and time requirements verified?
- Does a claim show cause, effect, entitlement and quantum rather than assertion alone?
- Were unsupported legal labels kept out?
- Would a hostile reader be able to quote a sentence as an admission or promise the user did not intend? If yes, rewrite it.

## Reference basis

This skill internalizes, rather than copies, professional guidance including:

- American Bar Association guidance on effective client emails: lead with important information, remain concise/focused, state recommendations and next steps/deadlines.
- Georgetown Law guidance on professional email communication: brief, direct, reader-centered and appropriately formal.
- FIDIC material emphasizing correctly worded contract correspondence, notice discipline, contemporaneous records and logically structured construction claims.

The purpose is not to reproduce proprietary precedent language. The purpose is to preserve the reasoning architecture that makes high-stakes professional writing reliable.
